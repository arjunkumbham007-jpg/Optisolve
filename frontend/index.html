Constraints (up to 100)

Coefficients may be any finite real numbers, including negative, fractional, zero, and values larger than 50. Decision variables are nonnegative. Standard Simplex requires ≤ constraints with nonnegative RHS; Two-Phase and Big-M support ≤, ≥, = and signed RHS values. Big-M may be numerically sensitive for poorly scaled models.

Variable bounds xᵢ ≥ 0 are built in. If you enter an explicit row such as x₁ ≥ 0 or x₂ ≥ 0, OptiSolve will recognize and ignore that redundant row automatically. Do not use this shortcut for a real constraint such as x₁ + x₂ ≥ 0.

An interactive linear programming solver

      
OptiSolve helps students explore linear programming (LP) models, compare optimization methods, and inspect how a numerical solution is reached. Enter objective coefficients and constraints directly, or describe explicit equations in words and review the extracted model before solving.

      
Explore Simplex, Two-Phase, and Big-M

      
The solver supports Standard Simplex for its supported model class, plus Two-Phase Simplex and Big-M for mixed inequality and equality constraints. Algorithm walkthroughs record tableaus, reduced-cost decisions, ratio tests, pivot operations, and phase transitions.

      
Visual explanations for optimization problems

      
For two-variable problems, OptiSolve can generate a graphical explanation of constraint boundaries, the feasible region, and the optimal point. A separate Manim animation visualizes the recorded algorithm trace. Higher-dimensional models receive numerical solutions and algorithm traces rather than a misleading two-dimensional graph.

      
OptiSolve is an educational tool. Review every model extracted from text, and independently verify important results before relying on them.
